## What they are

Like the Factory, the **Mill**, the **Bakery**, the **Dairy** and the **Storehouse** open an interior: a room where you place Fixtures. It earns no Takings and has no Visitors. An empty interior leaves the building exactly as it was; a fitted one adds a **bonus** that grows with the building's Tier. These interiors are a beta feature: switch on *Production interiors* in the Settings.

## One layout, one job

- The **Mill** turns on Drive alone: a Motor, Shafts and Gears bring a turning force to the **Millstones**, which speed up the grinding.
- The **Bakery** and the **Dairy** run a **Line**: crates travel on Conveyors from a Machine against the back wall, through the other Machines, to an Exit on the edge of the room. The Bakery's Mixer takes Drive and its Oven takes gas by Flow; the Dairy runs on Flow, from the Milk vat through the Pasteuriser to the Cheese cellar.
- The **Storehouse** runs a Line from a Loading dock, by a Hoist, to a Rack.

A Line only counts when it is unbroken from its first Machine to an Exit, and every Machine on it is linked. A cut, a missing Drive or gas, or a broken Fixture stops what lies downstream.

## Staff: who makes it pay

Each interior has its own roles, hired like the Staff of a Venue: a fee of five days of wages once, then a wage by the day. Every role delivers **its own part** of the effect, in proportion to the Citizens who actually come to work: a miller gives the speed, a baker the speed of the Bakery, a seller the pace of sales over its Counter, a cheesemaker the Cheese yield, a cream maker the Cream yield, a stocker the room of the Storehouse. A role you do not hire delivers nothing, but never takes away what the building did before.

A **Technician** repairs worn Fixtures by the hour and slows their wear, as in the Factory interior.

## What they cost

Motors draw electricity from the city, a fifth of it when they drive nothing. The Staff you hire are Jobs of the building, and their wages are paid every hour as far as your Urbs go.
