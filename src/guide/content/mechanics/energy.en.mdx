## Demand

Every Home needs power and water, more at each Tier. Workplaces need power too. The sum is the city's **Demand**. Power Demand may exceed production, and service then drops; water Demand never may: a <EntryLink id="waterTower" /> must keep up before the city can grow.

## Sources

| Source | Output | Cost and drawbacks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <EntryLink id="powerPlant" /> | Varies with the weather | Clean, nothing to run |
| <EntryLink id="solar" /> and <EntryLink id="solarHome" /> | Daytime only | Clean; panels first cover their own Home, then share with neighbours |
| <EntryLink id="hydroPlant" /> | Slowly varies over the Day cycle, scaled by the water around it | Clean; must touch Water tiles, full output with 8 connected tiles |
| <EntryLink id="geothermalPlant" /> | Constant, day and night | Clean, but expensive, unlocked late and limited in number |
| <EntryLink id="coalPlant" /> | Stable | Fuel paid in Urbs, emissions, lower Well-being around it; can be switched off |
| <EntryLink id="backup" /> | Only during a shortage | Paid in Urbs per unit supplied, polluting |

## Day and night

The map goes through a **Day cycle** that lasts 2 real hours: 16 hours of Daytime, then 8 of Night (the clock at the top shows its time, with a sun or a moon). Solar panels produce only in Daytime, strongest at midday; at Night, wind, water and ground heat carry the city, and a <EntryLink id="battery" /> can hold the solar surplus for it. The Day cycle only lights the map and shapes solar and water power: production times, taxes and every other rule keep counting real hours. A Time skip moves the Day cycle along with the clock. In the settings, **Always day** keeps the map lit as in Daytime without changing power.

## The supply order

Each moment, power is used in the same order:

1. **Renewables** first: wind, sun, water and ground heat.
2. **Coal** next, so that stored energy is kept for later.
3. **Neighborhood batteries** cover what is still missing. A <EntryLink id="battery" /> charges only from renewable surplus nearby, never from coal or backup.
4. **Backup** power plants close the remaining gap, at a price.

## Shortages

When power still falls short, Homes are served first; workplaces share what remains in proportion to their needs, and a Casino is cut before any of them. A short workplace produces more slowly. When a new shortage appears, an **Adaptation period** gives you time to react before it costs anything.

To lower Demand rather than raise production, insulate your Homes or equip them with solar panels.
