## Attractions

The Museums, the Colosseum, the stadiums, the Concert hall, the Theater, the Casino, the Marina, the Ferris wheel and the Roller coaster are **Attractions**. Each draws a number of **Tourists** a day, more at a higher Tier. A Hotel is not an Attraction: it lodges Tourists, who then stay and spend more, but it draws no one by itself.

## Ticket and Takings

Set the **Ticket** of an Attraction at one of three prices from its side panel. A higher Ticket earns more from each Tourist but draws fewer of them, which leaves your roads freer. Compare the three prices in the panel: it gives the Tourists and the Urbs per hour of each. On top of the Ticket, a Tourist spends on site. The Urbs build up as **Takings**, up to a cap that grows with the Tier, and you collect them by hand.

## Getting there

Tourists have no Home. They come by car from the edge of the city, or through a Railway station or Transit hub. A station within walking range of an Attraction sends part of them on foot, and public transport that covers the Attraction carries part of the rest. The ones who drive load your Roads, and those on foot load Sidewalks and Crossings. Select an Attraction in the Analytics view, on the Building flows Layer, to see where they come from.

## Shows

The Theater, the Concert hall and the Colosseum hold **Shows**. Choose when it starts, now or later: the panel states the crowd, the Ticket sales, the cost and the expected profit. Cancelled before it starts, a Show gives back half of its cost.

The crowd arrives in the hour before, stays for the Show and leaves in the hour after, when its Takings are paid to you. You can see it on the map, with a badge that gives the step and the time left. While it moves it loads the network and weighs a little on Well-being around the Attraction if most of it drives. You then have to wait before the next Show.

## Artists

In the Concert hall, pick one of the three **Artists** on offer. A higher **Fame** draws more people and earns more per Ticket, but costs a higher fee. Your city draws bigger names the more Citizens and Well-being it has. A big name can cost more than it earns when few people live nearby: look at the profit stated.

## Tourism at a glance

The **Tourism** section of City management sums up the Tourists, the earnings per hour, the Takings to collect, the events to come and what is best to do next. The more leisure places your city has, the more Tourists it draws.
