## When Waste starts to matter

Every Home produces Waste each day, in proportion to its Citizens. It only matters once the city has about 1,000 Citizens; a city that is already past that size gets an **Adaptation period** before Waste counts.

Waste is a Service, like education or health: a Home whose Waste is not processed lacks Waste coverage. That lowers its Well-being, can stop it from reaching a higher Tier, and costs Tax from Easy Difficulty.

## Three facilities

| Facility | Capacity | Drawbacks | Cost per unit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| <EntryLink id="recyclingPlant" /> | Medium, longest route | Needs power | Highest |
| <EntryLink id="incinerator" /> | Large, in a small footprint | Lowers nearby Well-being, adds emissions | Medium |
| <EntryLink id="mound" /> | Small: build many side by side | Its odor lowers Well-being around it | Lowest |

A Mound never fills up: it handles a daily amount, forever. The odor of several Mounds never adds up; a Home bears the worst one around it, so a landfill is a matter of where you put it.

## Collection follows the route

A Home is covered when a Waste facility has **capacity** left and a **route** joins them that is no longer than the facility's **Collection distance**, since trucks have to drive there. There is no circle around a facility: only the road counts. Each tile of road, BRT corridor or Pedestrian way adds one to the route. A BRT corridor counts as a road for this link: a Home beside one is joined to the roads the corridor touches or crosses. A Home reached only by Pedestrian ways is joined when its paths touch a linked road close enough, at a higher cost.

The Collection distance is 16 tiles for a Mound, 28 for an Incinerator and 50 for a Recycling plant. The longer the route, the more each unit costs: from the tariff next to the facility to twice the tariff at the Collection distance. The nearest Homes by route are served first. When a Home is not covered, its panel says why: capacity full, beyond the Collection distance, beyond the Carrying distance or no road link.

## Processing order

Each day, Waste goes to the cleanest facilities first: Recycling plants, then Incinerators, then Mounds, whatever they cost. You choose the mix by what you build, not with a setting. Each unit processed costs Urbs; a facility you cannot pay stops, and its Homes lose their coverage.

## Understanding a shortage

When Homes lack Waste coverage, the Waste section of the City management window explains why in a few lines, heaviest first, each with a button to show the place on the map:

- **No facility** at all: build one, and a Road to it.
- **Capacity short**: the city produces more than its facilities can process. Add facilities.
- **Facility stopped**: no Urbs left to pay it, no road link, or no power for a Recycling plant.
- **No road link**: Homes joined to a facility by no road.
- **Beyond the Collection distance**: Homes whose route to every facility is too long, or too far from its road along the Pedestrian paths.
- **Pedestrian surcharge**: Homes served only by Pedestrian ways cost more to collect. Give them a Road.
- **Odor and smoke**: Mounds and Incinerators lower the Well-being of the Homes around them.

## Fit out the Recycling plant

Open the interior of a Recycling plant from its side panel to fit out Recycling lines. A line is complete only when Waste goes from an **Intake** against the back wall, through a **Sorter**, then a **Recycler** (a **Shredder**, a **Smelting furnace** or a **Glass crusher**), to a **Collection exit** where a truck takes it away. Conveyors join the Machines, and every Machine is driven by a Motor, through Shafts and Gears.

- Each Collection exit on a complete line adds 50 units to the Waste the plant takes in each day, and 200 more when all three kinds of Recycler are at work.
- Each Recycler on a complete line adds 1.5 tiles to the Collection distance of the plant.
- The Machines wear: hire a Technician to repair them, and a broken Machine cuts the line behind it.
- A larger Tier has a larger room and allows more Exits, Sorters and Recyclers.
