## The loop of the game

Everything in Urbtopia follows one loop:

1. **Produce.** A <EntryLink id="workshop">Workshop</EntryLink> makes Materials, and a <EntryLink id="factory" /> turns Materials into Goods.
2. **Sell.** <EntryLink id="shop">Shops</EntryLink> sell Goods to your Citizens, and the Market buys the rest. Both pay in Urbs.
3. **Build.** Urbs pay for new buildings, roads and land.
4. **Grow.** A <EntryLink id="home" /> upgrades by consuming Goods, and a higher Tier houses more Citizens. Citizens pay Tax and unlock new buildings.

## Urbs

Urbs are the currency of the city. You earn them by selling Goods and by collecting the Tax of your Homes and the takings of your workplaces, and you spend them on building, upgrading and buying land.

## Citizens and unlocks

Citizens live in Homes, and their number depends on each Home's Tier. The total number of Citizens of your city is the key to progress: each building, Material and Good becomes available once the city reaches its threshold. Every Codex page shows the threshold of its building.

## Tiers

Most buildings have Tiers. Upgrading to a higher Tier costs Urbs, and sometimes Goods, and makes the building better: a Home houses more Citizens, a Workshop produces more, a <EntryLink id="storehouse" /> stores more.

## Power and water

Homes need power and water according to their Tier, and workplaces need power. Build a <EntryLink id="powerPlant" /> and a <EntryLink id="waterTower" /> early, and keep production ahead of Demand: see <MechanicsLink id="energy" />.

## Services and Well-being

Schools, hospitals, police and other public facilities cover the Homes around them. Covered Homes, nearby green spaces and smooth commutes raise Well-being, which raises Tax. The Difficulty of your city decides which missing services cost Tax: see <MechanicsLink id="sanctions" />.

## Time

The city keeps living while you are away: when you come back, everything that happened since your last visit is applied, up to 48 game hours.
