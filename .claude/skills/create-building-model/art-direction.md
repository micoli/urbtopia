# Art direction of the Urbix buildings

Read this file before designing or changing any building model. It records the decisions taken while rebuilding the civic buildings, the Homes and the towers in the `urbtopia` style. They define the look of every building of the game: follow them unless the user decides otherwise. Where a rule says "the user asked", it comes from a direct request.

How the work goes (process):

- The user decides each building one by one. Propose a direction in a few lines (silhouette, entrance, roof, balconies), wait for the answer, build it, show it, then wait for "commit".
- Show every trial in the user's live Blender (re-open the building's `.blend`, then set the 3D viewport to Material Preview shading and zoom on the building with `view_selected`, so the user sees it in their own viewport, then `look`) and not only as an image. Blender sometimes restarts on the default scene: check it first.
- Never commit before the user says so. A change asked after a commit is a new commit.
- Fix one thing per request. "Reduce a little" means a small step (a fifth to a quarter of the value); "halve" is exactly half. When a size is unclear, take the smallest visible change and say which value was used.
- Signs and any text on a model are in English.
- After a change to a recipe, regenerate the model (and its solar version), re-run the installation of the assets (`node scripts/install-assets.ts`, the game serves `public/models`, a stale copy hides the change) and the tests, then say what changed.

## Style and palette

- Style `urbtopia`: pale cool walls (`uwall`), mint roofs (`uroof`), thin plinth, no belt courses, wide windows, thin dark parapet on flat roofs, vector sign text in Space Grotesk.
- Roofs of civic buildings and flat roofs: a mid grey (`uslab`, `#b4b8c8`). Under the game's light a dark roof reads black and a very light one reads white.
- Homes come in four colors a to d: mint, blue-grey, brick red (with cream walls) and grey-green. A color is not a model: the model carries the colors of tone a, and a palette PNG per other tone (`<id>.tone-b.png` to `-d`) next to it recolors it live (Repaint swaps the palette texture). See "Color tones" below.
- Every building is drawn at the real size of its footprint (the footprint is the bounding box, roof and balconies included within a few hundredths). The game does not resize, tint or overlay a building flagged `drawnToScale`.
- The front of a model faces -Y in Blender; the game needs `rotationOffset: 180` with `scale: 1` on every model of this style.

## Silhouette rules

- No two buildings of a family look alike: each Tier gets its own silhouette, not the previous one with an extra floor.
- Prefer a hipped roof (`hip_ratio` 0.2: each end slopes over a fifth of the length) to a gable end on the main volume. A bay keeps a gable pointing out.
- The entrance is a small one-floor bay with a gable perpendicular to the main roof (`bay_floors` 1, `bay_clear_above`), not centered, with a small door hood on the door. A window on the facade next to it, never on the bay itself.
- A one-floor garage wing with a flat green roof (a few small bushes on it, `roof_plants`) breaks the symmetry on two-storey and taller houses. Wings and bays are joined (no seam, `overlap`) and flush on the same alignment.
- A round window may sit in the gable above the door, its bottom resting on the tip of the door hood (`on_hood`).
- A top floor set back with a terrace (glass wall on the half of the roof top, solid parapet in the wall color, four-slope roof on the penthouse) ends the tallest buildings that have a flat or mansard roof top. It cannot sit on a hipped roof.
- Roofs of towers are flat, flush with the facades (`roof_overhang` 0.01), the slab thickness equals the plinth (`slab_matches_plinth`), a thin green band marks each floor (`floor_bands`), the deck inside the parapet is light (`roof_deck`), with a stair head on one corner.

## Windows and balconies

- Windows are evenly spread. Extra windows are placed with `extra_windows` (face, position, level, width).
- A window above a bay is only at the levels the user asks for. The first floor above a bay stays blank.
- Balconies sit exactly under their window (`under_window`): same width plus 0.04, bottom at the sill.
- Balconies are staggered, never stacked in a regular grid: different levels on adjacent faces, alternating columns (`pick` first, last, outer, center), a checkerboard on the long sides of a long building.
- A balcony on the facade above a bay is placed with `at` and only when the user asks.
- "Second floor" counts levels above the ground floor (level 1 is the first floor, level 2 the second). If a request could mean another level, state which one was taken.
- "Left" and "right" of a face are given looking at that face from outside.

## Rules for the kit

- A new element goes into `scripts/building_lib.py` as an option or a feature, with a default that leaves every existing building byte-identical. Add it only when a building needs it.
- Never rename a model id (ADR 0022). Register with `scripts/register_model.py`, then `npm run defs:generate`.
- A model that a tier shows more than once is registered once and referenced by id.
- The user may ask to go back to a previous version: keep the recipe in the scratchpad generator and re-run it rather than editing models by hand.
- Parts that were tried and removed on request: chimneys on houses, a roof lantern on the ridge, a penthouse on the 8-floor tower, a balcony under the window above a bay unless asked.

## Shops (Shops, four Tiers, 1x1)

- Each Shop is one family of four Tiers; the Tier is the floor count (1 to 4). The ground floor is the same at every Tier of a family; only floors are added on top.
- Shared base: a door pushed to the left (`door_along`) under a marquee, one small bush right of the door (`bush_at`), a roof sign in English on a raised frame (the Shop's name: SHOP, FOOD, BUILD, HARDWARE, JEWELS), a thin band at each floor line when there are several floors.
- Upper floors take the windows of the previous family that the user validated, never a new pattern: a ribbon of glass per face (0.84 wide, no mullion), or the wide three-pane window of the Construction shop (0.62 wide, half a floor high) on the front and the back, and two classic windows per side. The user asked twice for ribbons running corner to corner on upper floors; keep the validated width unless asked to go wider.
- A picture window on the ground floor has no inner frame and goes low (`z_ratio` 0.12, `height_ratio` 0.7, one pane). Never a window in a bay.
- Each Shop has its own props, not a recolored copy: Grocery has a striped awning and a fruit stall, and a striped blind above the first-floor window at every upper floor of the front; Building shop has a gabled metal roof facing the street, a flat metal marquee, hung tools, a sand sack, a gravel sack and scattered planks, a window left of the door removed and replaced by one small window, two classic windows per side and a wide industrial garage door at the back with a yellow and dark band above; Hardware has a flat roof, a rack of three spaced dark toolboxes (handle, crossed tape on top and down the front and sides) with cable spools, and a flat spool on the roof; Jeweller has a gold and red roof border, a curved gold marquee at two thirds of the usual radius and lowered, a red carpet, two gold-capped pilasters and a crystal pedestal in the window, and no props on the roof.
- The name of a Shop in the game is not its category: Shop is the General shop, Grocery, Building shop, Hardware store, Jeweller. Signs use short English words close to the game name.
- Four color tones a to d per Tier, like the Homes: one model, one palette PNG per other tone.

## Production and utility buildings

- Each building is a family of Tiers; the Tier is shown by a count of a visible element, not by a new silhouette: the Workshop gains a chimney per Tier (one to five), the Factory a roof tank per Tier (one to five). The count is the Tier number.
- Workshop (2x2, one floor, floors 1.5 times taller via `floor_height_scale`): sawtooth roof, a roller door and an entrance door on the front, a roof sign raised on legs above the roof edge (the wall sign hides under the sawtooth overhang), a wide industrial garage door and nothing else on the back, three windows per side, chimneys in a row behind the sign.
- Factory (2x2, two floors, flat roof flush with the walls): a loading dock with a roller door and hazard edge in the middle of the front, hung tools on the left of the dock, one window on the right of it, a hazard band at the floor line, a ribbon window per face upstairs, two wide garage doors at the back, a roof sign, tanks in a row along the back wall, a run of ducts across the middle of the roof.
- Wind turbine (1x1, three Tiers, `build_turbine`): one single turbine at every Tier; the Tier is the number of mint rings on the mast (the first at a fifth of the mast height from the bottom, then every fifth). White tapered mast with a dark collar on a small mint disc base, a nacelle of the wall radius (0.03) cut in two colors along a slanted plane (white below, mint above with a rounded top), a mint hub, and three broad rotor blades white at the root and mint at the tip with a very light bevel. The rotor is large (radius 0.42), the base is small: the model does not fill the tile (0.69 x 0.4).
- Geothermal plant (1x1, three Tiers, `build_geothermal`): a grey pad, a rounded turbine hall (wall radius 0.03) with a mint band and a grey slab roof, a dark wellhead with a mint flange piped to the hall, and one flared cooling tower at the back right with a steam puff. The Tier is the height of the tower (0.55, 0.70, 0.85) and its number of mint rings (one to three, stacked from the top, each hugging the tower).

- Water tower (1x1, three Tiers, recipe `industrial-water-tower.py` builds the three): kept as the user drew it, with orange hoops on the tank, but the tank roof and the platform in the mid grey `uslab` (the first version used near-black, which the user found far too dark); the Tier is the platform height (0.74, 1.10, 1.40), the number of hoops (one to three) and the rungs of the ladder. - Roofs of production buildings keep half of the roof free, in front, for the Solar panel the game places there (see `solarPanels` in the building definition). Never put props where the definition places the panel.
- Heights of floors and signs on industrial roofs: raise the sign until its text is fully readable from the game camera.

- A Tier that does not exist yet keeps inheriting the model of the previous Tier in the game until it is drawn, so draw all the Tiers of a family before moving on.

## Accuracy of the workflow

- One level at a time: build it, show it in Blender, wait for "commit", then register the four colors, set the model on its Tier in the definition, run `npm run defs:generate`, install the assets, run the tests and read their result before committing.
- When a Kenney model becomes unused, remove it from `scripts/assetPacks.ts` and `assets/models.json`; a test fails otherwise. Tests that used the old Kenney model of a building for the repaint or the footprint are moved to another building still on the Kenney kit, or removed once none is left.
- Never commit files of a level the user has not validated: park them outside `assets/` until their turn.
- If Blender loses its connection, reopen with the single `.blend` of the model, not by importing several `.glb`; if it stays down, render with `tools/blender/render_views.py` or `game_render.py` and say so.
- Say plainly in the answer what is done, what is not committed, and any defect noticed on the render.

## Color tones (one model, recolored live)

- Never draw a color variant. A repaintable building is ONE model per Tier (id ending `-tone-a`, or `-mansard-a` for the old mansard Homes). The roles that change with the color use the role cells `vtone1` (accent and roof), `vtone2` (second accent, for instance the roof border or the darker hoops) and `vtone3` (walls, or a third color) of the `urbtopia` palette; every other color keeps its own cell.
- The recipe holds a `tones` table, `{"a": {"vtone1": "uroof", "vtone3": "uwall"}, "b": {...}, "c": {...}, "d": {...}}`, with palette names or `#rrggbb`. The model is built with the colors of tone a; the build also writes `<id>.tone-b.png`, `.tone-c.png` and `.tone-d.png` next to the `.glb` (the same palette image with the role cells recolored). The build also writes `<id>.tones.json` (the name and swatch color of each tone, from `scripts/tone_labels.py`: the first role cell of the table gives the color) and `register_model.py` copies it into `models.json` (`tones`); the game shows those names and swatches in the Repaint chips (`color.<name>` in the i18n files, add one for a new color name).
- In the game, `tones` on a model makes it repaintable: Repaint gives the building a `textureVariant` of the form `tone:<model file>:<b|c|d>` and the scene swaps the palette (nearest filter, no mipmaps). Models whose tones table is identical for every tone (the Packhouse) repaint without a visible change.
- A sign text keeps the ink chosen for tone a, whatever the tone.
- Adding a fifth color means adding a tone to the tables and to the `tones` list, not drawing models.
