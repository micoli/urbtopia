# Congestion as a core model with Vehicles as its projection

Accepted. Amends ADR 0005, which kept Traffic cosmetic. Road congestion now affects Well-being, so it lives in the pure injected-clock core: Commuters (Citizens not using public transport) are aggregated per Home, loaded on the shortest road path to workplaces, and compared with Lane capacity set by the Road tier. The bottleneck of each Commute drives a capped Well-being penalty, with an Adaptation period for saved cities. Vehicles in the scene remain a visual projection of this model: they never overlap, but individual Vehicles are not saved and carry no state of their own.

Individual Citizen journeys stay rejected, as in ADR 0005, to keep the simulation bounded and catch-up deterministic. Simulating queues in the scene and feeding congestion back to the core was rejected: it would make Well-being depend on rendering, frame rate and camera. Workplaces have no job limit for now; the aggregate Commute model leaves room to add one. Lane capacities, Road tier prices and the penalty cap are tunable in `.scratch/traffic-congestion/balancing.md`.

**Amendment: walking as a third aggregate mode.** Citizens also walk, and the model stays aggregate and pure. A Commuter whose workplace is reachable on the sidewalk graph under a distance threshold walks and no longer loads the road; Homes also generate walking trips to shops, schools, health, culture, casino and parks, which add no car demand but give Well-being. Sidewalks are implicit on every road tile with two sides, and a road is crossed only at a Crossing or around a dead end. A Crossing used by many pedestrians loses road capacity, so walkability trades against throughput. Pedestrians in the scene are, like Vehicles, a cosmetic projection: never saved, bounded in number, no feedback into the core, and cars stop visually while one crosses. Individual Citizen journeys stay rejected. Distances, weights and the capacity cut are tunable in `.scratch/pedestrian-network/balancing.md`.

**Amendment: buses are vehicles in traffic.** An active Bus line loads every tile of its route with a constant number of car equivalents and is slowed by the Congestion of that route: its Effective speed, then its capacity and itinerary times, follow the sum of per-tile delays (never below half of the nominal speed). The load does not depend on the speed, and the effective speeds are computed once, before Modal shift, so the calculation stays one pass and deterministic. BRT and rail keep their own networks and are not slowed. In the scene the bus joins the car-following engine and queues behind cars; cars spawn preferably on saturated roads so that the model's jams are visible. Ambulances, fire trucks and police cars keep their priority unless a configuration switch subjects them to the same engine. The scene still never feeds back into the core. Values are tunable in `.scratch/bus-in-traffic/balancing.md`.
